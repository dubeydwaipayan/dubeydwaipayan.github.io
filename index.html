About

My research explores the chemistry and physics of exoplanet atmospheres, with the broader goal of understanding how planetary atmospheres can inform us about planet formation and evolution. I am especially interested in the interpretation of elemental abundance ratios, atmospheric disequilibrium processes, and the extent to which observed spectra preserve reliable information about a planet’s origin and history.

As an astrophysicist working at the interface of exoplanet science, astrochemistry, and spectroscopy, I study these questions using theoretical and computational tools, including radiative transfer, thermochemical and disequilibrium chemistry, and Bayesian retrieval techniques. My work aims not only to model atmospheric processes, but also to understand how those processes manifest in real observations, and which inferred trends are physically robust versus method-dependent.

My current research focuses on the atmospheric chemistry of gas giants and sub-Neptunes, particularly in the context of JWST and comparative exoplanet spectroscopy. I am interested in building more physically informed retrieval frameworks that connect observed spectra to atmospheric composition in a reliable way, while reducing biases introduced by oversimplified assumptions.
